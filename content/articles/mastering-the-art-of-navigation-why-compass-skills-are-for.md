## A Lost Art
Your GPS device is dead, and you're deep in the wilderness with no signal in sight. You're not alone – this scenario plays out for many hikers every year. The culprit? Overreliance on technology. But there's a solution that's been around for centuries: the humble compass. 
Compasses aren't just a relic of the past; they're a practical safety tool that can mean the difference between life and death. And yet, many hikers are abandoning this essential skill in favor of their smartphones.

## The Protocol
To navigate with a compass, you'll need to understand a few key concepts. First, there's the difference between magnetic and true north. Magnetic north is where the compass needle points, while true north is the Earth's actual rotational axis. The difference between the two is known as declination, and it varies depending on your location. 
Next, you'll need to learn how to hold the compass correctly. This might seem obvious, but it's essential to hold the compass level and away from any metal objects that could interfere with the needle. 
Now, let's talk about taking a bearing. This involves rotating the bezel (the outer ring of the compass) until the direction-of-travel arrow points towards your destination. The bearing will be the number on the bezel that lines up with the magnetic needle.

## Gear Loadout
When it comes to choosing a compass, there are a few things to keep in mind. First, look for a compass with a sturdy build and a clear, easy-to-read dial. Some compasses also come with additional features like a clinometer (for measuring slope angle) or a mirror (for signaling). 
A good compass should also have a reliable and accurate needle. Look for a compass with a high-quality magnet that won't be affected by temperature changes or other environmental factors. 
Some popular compass models include the Suunto A-10 and the Silva Ranger. Both of these compasses are known for their accuracy and durability.

## Field Execution
Now that you've got your compass, it's time to put it to use. Start by setting your declination. This will ensure that your compass is accurate and reliable. 
Next, take a bearing on a landmark or trail junction. This will help you get a sense of direction and ensure that you're on the right track. 
As you hike, be sure to take regular bearings and check your progress. This will help you stay on track and avoid getting lost.

## Ranger Secrets
One of the most common mistakes hikers make when using a compass is not accounting for declination. This can result in a bearing that's off by several degrees, which can be disastrous in the wilderness. 
Another mistake is not using a baseplate compass. These compasses have a flat base that allows you to draw a straight line along a map, making it easier to take bearings and navigate. 
Finally, don't forget to practice using your compass before you head out on a hike. This will help you build muscle memory and ensure that you're comfortable using the compass in a variety of situations.

Be prepared, and always carry a compass – your life may depend on it.
